## Intermediate results

The initial copy already has the right basic sequence: saved video, project relevance, plan, and PR. The main risks are implying every source becomes a useful change, suggesting broad platform access, and treating a specified route as enabled. The library-first action is appropriate because users can collect content before connecting GitHub. A labeled no-fit demonstration would help explain selectivity.

The research training's most useful instruction is to inspect what people already use and preserve their own language. Here that produced both save-and-forget examples and an objection from a person who does not want saving treated as a problem. The conclusion is a behavior-defined developer segment, not all social-media users.

Competitor research changed the argument. Raindrop already advertises searchable YouTube speech. Readwise supports improved synchronized transcripts. SavedThat is a close cross-platform video-library rival. GitHub's current cloud agent researches repositories, plans, changes code, and creates PRs. VibeScroller should demonstrate the continuous source, decision, plan, and outcome record rather than claim to be the first summarizer or agent.

The Agora transcript's applicable lesson is to explain why the workflow matters before polishing headlines. The draft argument allows no-fit results, existing-tool alternatives, and a rational decision not to buy. Its evidence cannot support revenue guarantees, fixed savings, or a proprietary monopoly.
