## Big idea

"A saved idea becomes useful when you can decide what it means for the project you are building."

This is the proposed argument. A clip can be interesting and still irrelevant, already implemented, or risky. VibeScroller aims to help make that distinction, preserve the source, and prepare a next step the user can inspect. Whether the service does this better than a manual stack remains a pilot question.

## Metaphor

## Potential unique mechanism of the problem

The proposed mechanism is a missing project-review step. A saved link remembers the source. It does not by itself evaluate the technique against the current repository, priorities, and constraints. A generic summary can preserve useful information while leaving that work with the user.

This is a hypothesis about the proposed segment. It is not proof that bookmarks fail, a universal cause of inactivity, or a psychological diagnosis. Public dissent shows some people do not want every save turned into work.

## Potential unique mechanism of the solution

The proposed mechanism is an evidence-linked review against a selected repository snapshot. The service keeps the source and bounded transcript or frame evidence, compares the idea with recorded project context, and explains a possible change or a reason to decline. It then creates a plan the user can edit. Separate approval authorizes coding with a specified executor and funding ceiling. A draft PR preserves the implementation for review.

The distinction is the complete source-to-outcome record. Its uniqueness is unverified and its superiority is unmeasured. V1's MIT intent also makes "secret proprietary method" a poor description. Market the inspectable workflow, not an invented technical monopoly.
